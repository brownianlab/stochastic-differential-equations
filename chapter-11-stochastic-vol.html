Chapter 11 — Local and Stochastic Volatility

Guiding question

Black–Scholes assumes constant volatility. Markets produce an implied volatility surface that varies by both strike and maturity — the "volatility smile." What extensions of GBM reproduce this surface, and what are the trade-offs between local and stochastic volatility models?

The empirical failure of Black–Scholes is specific: the implied volatility σimp(K,T)\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)σimp​(K,T) extracted from market option prices is not flat. Out-of-the-money puts trade at higher implied vol than at-the-money options (the skew), and implied vol varies with maturity (the term structure). Two classes of models address this:

  1. Local volatility (Dupire): σ\sigmaσ depends on StS_tSt​ and ttt, not on a separate random factor.
  2. Stochastic volatility (Heston, SABR): σt\sigma_tσt​ is driven by its own SDE.

11.1 The implied volatility surface​

Given market prices Cmkt(K,T)C_{\rm mkt}(K,T)Cmkt​(K,T) for all strikes KKK and maturities TTT, the implied volatility σimp(K,T)\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)σimp​(K,T) is the unique σ\sigmaσ in Black–Scholes that matches each price:

CBS(S0,K,T,r,σimp(K,T))=Cmkt(K,T).C_{\rm BS}(S_0,K,T,r,\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)) = C_{\rm mkt}(K,T).CBS​(S0​,K,T,r,σimp​(K,T))=Cmkt​(K,T).

The surface σimp(K,T)\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)σimp​(K,T) is the market's consensus view of future realized volatility, embedded in option prices. Key features:

  • Skew: ∂Kσimp<0\partial_K\sigma_{\rm imp} < 0∂K​σimp​<0 for equities (puts more expensive than calls at same distance from ATM).
  • Smile: σimp\sigma_{\rm imp}σimp​ is U-shaped in KKK for many asset classes (FX, rates).
  • Term structure: σimp\sigma_{\rm imp}σimp​ changes with TTT.

11.2 Local volatility: Dupire's formula​

Assume the stock follows:

dSt=rSt dt+σloc(t,St) St dWt.dS_t = r S_t\,dt + \sigma_{\rm loc}(t,S_t)\,S_t\,dW_t.dSt​=rSt​dt+σloc​(t,St​)St​dWt​.

The local volatility function σloc(t,S)\sigma_{\rm loc}(t,S)σloc​(t,S) is a deterministic function of time and spot. This model is complete — every option payoff is replicable.

Dupire's equation

Given a complete set of European call prices C(K,T)C(K,T)C(K,T) that are free of arbitrage, the unique local volatility that reproduces them is:

σloc2(T,K)=∂TC+rK∂KC12K2∂KKC.\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(T,K) = \frac{\partial_T C + rK\partial_K C}{\tfrac12 K^2\partial_{KK}C}.σloc2​(T,K)=21​K2∂KK​C∂T​C+rK∂K​C​.
Derivation from the forward Kolmogorov equation

The call price satisfies the Dupire forward PDE (in terms of KKK and TTT, not SSS and ttt):

∂TC=12σloc2(T,K)K2∂KKC−rK∂KC.\partial_T C = \tfrac12\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(T,K)K^2\partial_{KK}C - rK\partial_K C.∂T​C=21​σloc2​(T,K)K2∂KK​C−rK∂K​C.

Solving for σloc2\sigma_{\rm loc}^2σloc2​ gives the formula above. This is Kolmogorov's forward equation written for the European call as a function of its boundary data (K,T)(K,T)(K,T).

Local vol as conditional expectation of realized variance

The local variance σloc2(t,K)\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,K)σloc2​(t,K) equals the expected instantaneous variance of the stock, conditional on St=KS_t = KSt​=K:

σloc2(t,K)=E[σt2∣St=K].\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,K) = \mathbb E[\sigma_t^2\mid S_t = K].σloc2​(t,K)=E[σt2​∣St​=K].

This holds in any model where σt\sigma_tσt​ is a function of (t,St)(t,S_t)(t,St​) or is stochastic. Local vol is the "smile-consistent" variance — it is the exact variance needed to match option prices, but says nothing about how variance moves in the future.

Limitation of local vol. Local volatility models fit the current surface exactly, but their dynamics are unrealistic: forward implied vols are "sticky delta" in the wrong direction (empirically, vol tends to rise when SSS falls more than local vol predicts). They also produce dynamics where the ATM vol smile flattens too fast as TTT increases.

11.3 Stochastic volatility: the Heston model​

The Heston model adds a mean-reverting variance process:

dSt=rSt dt+Vt St dWtS,dS_t = rS_t\,dt + \sqrt{V_t}\,S_t\,dW_t^S,dSt​=rSt​dt+Vt​​St​dWtS​, dVt=κ(θ−Vt) dt+ξVt dWtV,dV_t = \kappa(\theta - V_t)\,dt + \xi\sqrt{V_t}\,dW_t^V,dVt​=κ(θ−Vt​)dt+ξVt​​dWtV​, d[WS,WV]t=ρ dt.d[W^S, W^V]_t = \rho\,dt.d[WS,WV]t​=ρdt.

Here VtV_tVt​ is the instantaneous variance (so Vt\sqrt{V_t}Vt​​ is the instantaneous volatility), θ\thetaθ is the long-run variance, κ\kappaκ is the mean-reversion speed, ξ\xiξ is the vol-of-vol, and ρ\rhoρ is the correlation.

Heston stochastic volatility model
Heston: vol is stochastic, mean-reverting. ρ < 0 (leverage effect) creates the implied volatility skew: OTM puts are expensive, OTM calls are cheap. Increasing ξ (vol-of-vol) steepens the smile. Feller condition 2κθ > ξ² keeps vol strictly positive.

Feller condition: 2κθ>ξ22\kappa\theta > \xi^22κθ>ξ2 ensures Vt>0V_t > 0Vt​>0 a.s. (Chapter 5).

Risk-neutral measure. The Heston model has one stock and two Brownian motions. The market is incomplete: there is a one-parameter family of risk-neutral measures indexed by the market price of variance risk λ\lambdaλ. Typically one sets λ=0\lambda = 0λ=0 (zero variance risk premium) or calibrates λ\lambdaλ from the term structure of implied vol.

11.5 The implied volatility smile from Heston​

The Heston model produces a characteristic smile:

ParameterEffect on smile
ρ<0\rho < 0ρ<0Left skew (equity-like): high vol when SSS falls
ρ>0\rho > 0ρ>0Right skew (FX-like): high vol when SSS rises
ξ\xiξ largeWider smile (more curvature)
κ\kappaκ largeFlatter long-term smile (fast mean-reversion)
θ\thetaθ largeHigher overall vol level
Leverage effect and ρ < 0

Empirically, equity implied vol rises sharply when the market falls. This "leverage effect" means ρ<0\rho < 0ρ<0: negative correlation between price moves and volatility moves. In the Heston model, ρ\rhoρ is the correlation between dWSdW^SdWS and dWVdW^VdWV. A fall in SSS (driven by dWS<0dW^S < 0dWS<0) coincides with a rise in VVV (driven by dWV>0dW^V > 0dWV>0 when ρ<0\rho < 0ρ<0), reproducing the observed negative equity skew.

11.6 SABR model​

The SABR (Stochastic Alpha Beta Rho) model is widely used in interest rates:

dFt=αtFtβ dWt,dαt=ναt dW^t,d[W,W^]=ρ dt.dF_t = \alpha_t F_t^\beta\,dW_t, \qquad d\alpha_t = \nu\alpha_t\,d\hat W_t, \qquad d[W,\hat W] = \rho\,dt.dFt​=αt​Ftβ​dWt​,dαt​=ναt​dW^t​,d[W,W^]=ρdt.

For β=1\beta = 1β=1: SABR with lognormal backbone. For β=0\beta = 0β=0: normal SABR.

The Hagan–Kumar–Lesniewski–Woodward (HKLW) approximation gives an explicit formula for implied vol in terms of (F,K,T,α,β,ν,ρ)(F, K, T, \alpha, \beta, \nu, \rho)(F,K,T,α,β,ν,ρ):

The formula is used to interpolate the vol surface from calibrated SABR parameters.

11.7 Local stochastic volatility (LSV)​

A practical compromise between local vol (exact fit) and stochastic vol (realistic dynamics):

dSt=rSt dt+σloc(t,St)Vt St dWtS.dS_t = rS_t\,dt + \sigma_{\rm loc}(t,S_t)\sqrt{V_t}\,S_t\,dW_t^S.dSt​=rSt​dt+σloc​(t,St​)Vt​​St​dWtS​.

The function σloc\sigma_{\rm loc}σloc​ is chosen so the model exactly reproduces the market surface, while VtV_tVt​ provides stochastic dynamics. The calibration condition is:

σloc2(t,K)=σDupire2(t,K)E[Vt∣St=K].\sigma_{\rm loc}^2(t,K) = \frac{\sigma_{\rm Dupire}^2(t,K)}{\mathbb E[\sqrt{V_t}\mid S_t = K]}.σloc2​(t,K)=E[Vt​​∣St​=K]σDupire2​(t,K)​.

This requires a Fokker–Planck PDE for the joint density of (St,Vt)(S_t,V_t)(St​,Vt​).
