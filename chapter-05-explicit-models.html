Chapter 5 — Explicitly Solvable Models

Guiding question

Which SDEs can be solved in closed form? What techniques reduce a nonlinear SDE to a linear one, and what do the solutions reveal about mean-reversion, fat tails, and the geometry of financial dynamics?

Most SDEs require numerical simulation. But a small, important family can be solved exactly using Itô's formula and algebraic tricks. These exact solutions are the calibration targets for more complex models and the benchmarks for numerical schemes.

Three canonical models appear throughout quantitative finance:

5.1 Geometric Brownian Motion​

We derived in Chapter 3 that

dSt=μSt dt+σSt dWt,S0>0,dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t,\qquad S_0 > 0,dSt​=μSt​dt+σSt​dWt​,S0​>0,

has the explicit solution

St=S0exp⁡ ⁣[(μ−12σ2)t+σWt].S_t = S_0\exp\!\left[\left(\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\right)t + \sigma W_t\right].St​=S0​exp[(μ−21​σ2)t+σWt​].

Let us now study this solution carefully.

Log-normal distribution. Since Wt∼N(0,t)W_t\sim N(0,t)Wt​∼N(0,t), we have

log⁡St∼N ⁣(log⁡S0+(μ−12σ2)t, σ2t).\log S_t \sim N\!\left(\log S_0 + (\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)t,\ \sigma^2 t\right).logSt​∼N(logS0​+(μ−21​σ2)t, σ2t).

The price StS_tSt​ is log-normally distributed: St=eNormalS_t = e^{\text{Normal}}St​=eNormal.

Mean and variance.

E[St]=S0eμt,Var(St)=S02e2μt(eσ2t−1).\mathbb E[S_t] = S_0 e^{\mu t}, \qquad \text{Var}(S_t) = S_0^2 e^{2\mu t}(e^{\sigma^2 t}-1).E[St​]=S0​eμt,Var(St​)=S02​e2μt(eσ2t−1).

Median.

Median(St)=S0e(μ−σ2/2)t,\text{Median}(S_t) = S_0 e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)t},Median(St​)=S0​e(μ−σ2/2)t,

which is less than the mean for all σ>0\sigma > 0σ>0. This is Jensen's inequality: E[eX]≥eE[X]\mathbb E[e^X] \ge e^{\mathbb E[X]}E[eX]≥eE[X] for convex functions.

Volatility drag: why higher vol hurts median growth

Imagine two assets, both with μ=10%\mu = 10\%μ=10%. Asset A has σ=10%\sigma = 10\%σ=10%, Asset B has σ=40%\sigma = 40\%σ=40%.

After T=10T=10T=10 years:

  • E[ST/S0]=e0.1×10≈2.72\mathbb E[S_T/S_0] = e^{0.1\times 10} \approx 2.72E[ST​/S0​]=e0.1×10≈2.72 (same for both)
  • Median A: e(0.10−0.005)×10=e0.95≈2.59e^{(0.10-0.005)\times 10} = e^{0.95}\approx 2.59e(0.10−0.005)×10=e0.95≈2.59
  • Median B: e(0.10−0.08)×10=e0.20≈1.22e^{(0.10-0.08)\times 10} = e^{0.20}\approx 1.22e(0.10−0.08)×10=e0.20≈1.22

Asset B's expected value is the same, but more than half the scenarios produce less than 22% gain over 10 years. High volatility depresses the typical outcome even when the mean is unchanged. This is the practical meaning of the −σ2/2-\sigma^2/2−σ2/2 Itô correction.

Geometric Brownian Motion: S_t = S₀ exp((μ − σ²/2)t + σW_t)
GBM log-returns are normal: log(S_t/S_0) ~ N((μ−σ²/2)t, σ²t). Notice that higher σ widens the band but also drags the median below the mean — Jensen's inequality in action.

Return autocorrelation. Log-returns rk=log⁡(Stk+1/Stk)r_k = \log(S_{t_{k+1}}/S_{t_k})rk​=log(Stk+1​​/Stk​​) are i.i.d. N((μ−σ2/2)Δt,σ2Δt)N((\mu-\sigma^2/2)\Delta t, \sigma^2\Delta t)N((μ−σ2/2)Δt,σ2Δt). No autocorrelation, by the independence of Brownian increments.

5.2 The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process​

OU process: explicit solution

The SDE

dXt=κ(θ−Xt) dt+σ dWt,X0=x0,dX_t = \kappa(\theta - X_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t, \qquad X_0 = x_0,dXt​=κ(θ−Xt​)dt+σdWt​,X0​=x0​,

has the unique strong solution

Xt=θ+(x0−θ)e−κt+σ∫0te−κ(t−s) dWs.X_t = \theta + (x_0-\theta)e^{-\kappa t} + \sigma\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\,dW_s.Xt​=θ+(x0​−θ)e−κt+σ∫0t​e−κ(t−s)dWs​.
Derivation via integrating factor

Define Yt=eκt(Xt−θ)Y_t = e^{\kappa t}(X_t - \theta)Yt​=eκt(Xt​−θ). By Itô's product rule:

dYt=κeκt(Xt−θ) dt+eκtdXt.dY_t = \kappa e^{\kappa t}(X_t-\theta)\,dt + e^{\kappa t}dX_t.dYt​=κeκt(Xt​−θ)dt+eκtdXt​.

Substituting dXt=κ(θ−Xt) dt+σ dWtdX_t = \kappa(\theta-X_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_tdXt​=κ(θ−Xt​)dt+σdWt​:

dYt=eκt[κ(Xt−θ) dt+κ(θ−Xt) dt+σ dWt]=σeκt dWt.dY_t = e^{\kappa t}[\kappa(X_t-\theta)\,dt + \kappa(\theta-X_t)\,dt + \sigma\,dW_t] = \sigma e^{\kappa t}\,dW_t.dYt​=eκt[κ(Xt​−θ)dt+κ(θ−Xt​)dt+σdWt​]=σeκtdWt​.

Integrating: Yt=Y0+σ∫0teκs dWsY_t = Y_0 + \sigma\int_0^t e^{\kappa s}\,dW_sYt​=Y0​+σ∫0t​eκsdWs​.

Converting back: Xt−θ=e−κtYt=(x0−θ)e−κt+σ∫0te−κ(t−s) dWsX_t - \theta = e^{-\kappa t}Y_t = (x_0-\theta)e^{-\kappa t} + \sigma\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)}\,dW_sXt​−θ=e−κtYt​=(x0​−θ)e−κt+σ∫0t​e−κ(t−s)dWs​.

Distribution. Since the stochastic integral is a Gaussian with mean zero and variance σ2∫0te−2κ(t−s) ds=σ22κ(1−e−2κt)\sigma^2\int_0^t e^{-2\kappa(t-s)}\,ds = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}(1-e^{-2\kappa t})σ2∫0t​e−2κ(t−s)ds=2κσ2​(1−e−2κt):

Xt∼N ⁣(θ+(x0−θ)e−κt, σ22κ(1−e−2κt)).X_t \sim N\!\left(\theta + (x_0-\theta)e^{-\kappa t},\ \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}(1-e^{-2\kappa t})\right).Xt​∼N(θ+(x0​−θ)e−κt, 2κσ2​(1−e−2κt)).

Stationary distribution. As t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞, XtX_tXt​ converges in distribution to

X∞∼N ⁣(θ, σ22κ).X_\infty \sim N\!\left(\theta,\ \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}\right).X∞​∼N(θ, 2κσ2​).

This is the unique stationary distribution of the OU process.

Mean reversion as exponential decay

The deterministic part of the solution is (x0−θ)e−κt(x_0-\theta)e^{-\kappa t}(x0​−θ)e−κt: the displacement from the long-run mean θ\thetaθ decays exponentially at rate κ\kappaκ. The half-life of deviations is log⁡2/κ\log 2/\kappalog2/κ. The stochastic part is the accumulated noise weighted by the exponential kernel e−κ(t−s)e^{-\kappa(t-s)}e−κ(t−s) — older noise matters less. The OU process is a memory-fading random process: its past is discounted, not forgotten.

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck: dX = κ(θ − X)dt + σ dW
All paths are pulled toward θ at rate κ. Increasing κ tightens the band and speeds mean-reversion. The stationary distribution is N(θ, σ²/2κ). In finance, κ models interest-rate mean reversion speed.

Autocovariance. For s≤ts\le ts≤t:

Cov(Xs,Xt)=σ22κe−κ(t−s)(1−e−2κs).\text{Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa(t-s)}(1-e^{-2\kappa s}).Cov(Xs​,Xt​)=2κσ2​e−κ(t−s)(1−e−2κs).

In the stationary regime (s→∞s\to\inftys→∞, t−s=τt-s = \taut−s=τ fixed):

Cov(Xs+τ,Xs)≈σ22κe−κτ.\text{Cov}(X_{s+\tau},X_s) \approx \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}e^{-\kappa\tau}.Cov(Xs+τ​,Xs​)≈2κσ2​e−κτ.

The autocovariance decays exponentially — a characteristic signature of mean reversion.

OU in finance: log-volatility, spreads, convenience yields

The OU process models quantities that fluctuate around a long-run level:

  • Interest rates (Vasicek model): short rate rtr_trt​ reverts to θ\thetaθ
  • Log-volatility in rough/stochastic vol models: log⁡σt\log\sigma_tlogσt​ follows OU
  • Commodity convenience yield: the net benefit of holding physical commodity
  • Credit spreads: OU on spreads captures mean reversion after credit events

The key feature is that extreme values are self-correcting: high values have negative expected drift, low values have positive expected drift.

5.3 The CIR process​

The Cox–Ingersoll–Ross (CIR) process is:

dXt=κ(θ−Xt) dt+σXt dWt,X0>0.dX_t = \kappa(\theta - X_t)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt{X_t}\,dW_t, \qquad X_0 > 0.dXt​=κ(θ−Xt​)dt+σXt​​dWt​,X0​>0.

Unlike OU, the diffusion coefficient σXt\sigma\sqrt{X_t}σXt​​ vanishes at Xt=0X_t = 0Xt​=0, which prevents the process from going negative (under the Feller condition).

Feller condition for positivity

If 2κθ≥σ22\kappa\theta \ge \sigma^22κθ≥σ2 (the Feller condition), then Xt>0X_t > 0Xt​>0 for all ttt, almost surely.

If 2κθ<σ22\kappa\theta < \sigma^22κθ<σ2, then XtX_tXt​ touches zero and reflects, but the SDE remains well-posed.

Why the CIR diffusion coefficient matters. In the OU process, noise is constant: σ dW\sigma\,dWσdW. In CIR, noise scales as σXt dW\sigma\sqrt{X_t}\,dWσXt​​dW: when XtX_tXt​ is large, noise is proportionally larger; when Xt→0X_t\to 0Xt​→0, noise vanishes, and the drift κθ>0\kappa\theta > 0κθ>0 pushes the process back up.

The Feller boundary at zero

The boundary {X=0}\{X=0\}{X=0} is a reflecting boundary for the CIR process when the Feller condition fails (2κθ<σ22\kappa\theta < \sigma^22κθ<σ2), or an unattainable boundary when it holds. The scaling X\sqrt{X}X​ of the diffusion near zero creates a repulsion from the boundary that is precisely calibrated by the Feller condition: when the drift κθ\kappa\thetaκθ is strong enough relative to σ2\sigma^2σ2, the boundary is never reached.

Distribution. The conditional distribution of XtX_tXt​ given X0=x0X_0=x_0X0​=x0​ is a scaled non-central chi-squared:

4κσ2(1−e−κt) Xt∼χ2 ⁣(4κθσ2, λ),\frac{4\kappa}{\sigma^2(1-e^{-\kappa t})}\,X_t \sim \chi^2\!\left(\frac{4\kappa\theta}{\sigma^2},\ \lambda\right),σ2(1−e−κt)4κ​Xt​∼χ2(σ24κθ​, λ),

where λ=4κx0e−κtσ2(1−e−κt)\lambda = \frac{4\kappa x_0 e^{-\kappa t}}{\sigma^2(1-e^{-\kappa t})}λ=σ2(1−e−κt)4κx0​e−κt​ is the non-centrality parameter.

Stationary distribution. As t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞, XtX_tXt​ converges to a Gamma distribution:

X∞∼Gamma ⁣(2κθσ2, σ22κ).X_\infty \sim \text{Gamma}\!\left(\frac{2\kappa\theta}{\sigma^2},\ \frac{\sigma^2}{2\kappa}\right).X∞​∼Gamma(σ22κθ​, 2κσ2​).

Mean =θ= \theta=θ, variance =σ2θ2κ= \frac{\sigma^2\theta}{2\kappa}=2κσ2θ​.

CIR in finance: interest rates and variance

Vasicek/CIR interest rates: The short rate rtr_trt​ in the CIR model stays positive (under the Feller condition), unlike Vasicek where negative rates are possible. CIR became a benchmark short-rate model for interest rate derivatives.

Heston stochastic volatility: The variance process VtV_tVt​ in the Heston model satisfies dV=κ(θ−V) dt+ξV dWvdV = \kappa(\theta-V)\,dt + \xi\sqrt{V}\,dW^vdV=κ(θ−V)dt+ξV​dWv. The Feller condition 2κθ>ξ22\kappa\theta > \xi^22κθ>ξ2 ensures Vt>0V_t > 0Vt​>0, keeping volatility well-defined.

5.4 Linear SDEs and the integrating factor method​

The technique that solved the OU process generalizes. A linear SDE has the form:

dXt=[a(t)+b(t)Xt] dt+[c(t)+d(t)Xt] dWt.dX_t = [a(t) + b(t)X_t]\,dt + [c(t) + d(t)X_t]\,dW_t.dXt​=[a(t)+b(t)Xt​]dt+[c(t)+d(t)Xt​]dWt​.

The solution uses the stochastic integrating factor Φt\Phi_tΦt​ satisfying

dΦt=−b(t)Φt dt−d(t)Φt dWt.d\Phi_t = -b(t)\Phi_t\,dt - d(t)\Phi_t\,dW_t.dΦt​=−b(t)Φt​dt−d(t)Φt​dWt​.

By Itô's formula, this exponential process has the form

Φt=exp⁡ ⁣(−∫0tbs ds−∫0tds dWs−12∫0tds2 ds),\Phi_t = \exp\!\left(-\int_0^t b_s\,ds - \int_0^t d_s\,dW_s - \tfrac12\int_0^t d_s^2\,ds\right),Φt​=exp(−∫0t​bs​ds−∫0t​ds​dWs​−21​∫0t​ds2​ds),

and d(ΦtXt)=Φt c(t) dWt+Φt a(t) dtd(\Phi_t X_t) = \Phi_t\,c(t)\,dW_t + \Phi_t\,a(t)\,dtd(Φt​Xt​)=Φt​c(t)dWt​+Φt​a(t)dt, which can then be integrated.

5.8 What this chapter gives you​

The three canonical models encode three fundamentally different behaviors:

  • GBM: multiplicative noise, log-normal distribution, no mean reversion, used for asset prices.
  • OU: additive noise, Gaussian distribution, exponential mean reversion, used for rates and spreads.
  • CIR: multiplicative noise scaled by X\sqrt{X}X​, gamma stationary distribution, positivity preservation, used for variances and rates.

Each is solved by the integrating factor method, which generalizes to all linear SDEs. The next chapter introduces the change-of-measure technique — Girsanov's theorem — which is the analytical foundation of risk-neutral pricing.
