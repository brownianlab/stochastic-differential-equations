Chapter 4 — SDE Definition and Existence

Guiding question

Given a drift function b(t,x)b(t,x)b(t,x) and a diffusion function σ(t,x)\sigma(t,x)σ(t,x), does the stochastic differential equation dXt=b(t,Xt) dt+σ(t,Xt) dWtdX_t = b(t,X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t,X_t)\,dW_tdXt​=b(t,Xt​)dt+σ(t,Xt​)dWt​ have a solution? Is it unique? What conditions on bbb and σ\sigmaσ are needed?

An SDE is a prescription for how a process evolves in time: a deterministic drift plus a random diffusion. The existence question asks whether such a prescription is self-consistent — whether a process with the prescribed dynamics can actually be constructed. Uniqueness asks whether the dynamics determine the process completely.

4.1 The SDE as an integral equation​

The shorthand dXt=b(t,Xt) dt+σ(t,Xt) dWtdX_t = b(t,X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t,X_t)\,dW_tdXt​=b(t,Xt​)dt+σ(t,Xt​)dWt​ is notation for the integral equation:

Xt=X0+∫0tb(s,Xs) ds+∫0tσ(s,Xs) dWs,0≤t≤T.X_t = X_0 + \int_0^t b(s,X_s)\,ds + \int_0^t \sigma(s,X_s)\,dW_s, \qquad 0\le t\le T.Xt​=X0​+∫0t​b(s,Xs​)ds+∫0t​σ(s,Xs​)dWs​,0≤t≤T.

The first integral is a Lebesgue integral (pathwise, since bbb is a drift), and the second is an Itô integral. Finding XXX means finding a continuous adapted process that satisfies this identity for all ttt.

The SDE is implicit

The unknown process XXX appears inside the integrals on the right side. This makes the SDE an implicit equation. The existence theorem constructs XXX as the fixed point of a contraction map in the L2L^2L2 sense.

4.2 Strong and weak solutions​

There are two notions of solution.

A strong solution is a process XXX defined on the given probability space (Ω,F,P)(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)(Ω,F,P), adapted to the filtration generated by WWW, that satisfies the integral equation pathwise.

A weak solution is a triple (Ω,F,P,W,X)(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P, W, X)(Ω,F,P,W,X) — possibly a new probability space with a new Brownian motion — on which the integral equation holds.

Every strong solution is a weak solution. The converse is false in general.

Analogy with ODEs

For an ODE x˙=f(x)\dot x = f(x)x˙=f(x), a strong solution constructs the trajectory from the given vector field fff and starting point. A weak solution allows changing the underlying space (redefining what "random" means) as long as the marginal dynamics are preserved.

In finance, the distinction matters for hedging: a strong solution gives a trading strategy that replicates under the original measure, while a weak solution only gives pricing without a hedging prescription.

4.3 Lipschitz conditions​

The standard hypothesis for existence and uniqueness of strong solutions is:

Standard conditions (Lipschitz and linear growth)

Suppose b,σ:[0,T]×R→Rb,\sigma:[0,T]\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb Rb,σ:[0,T]×R→R satisfy:

  1. Lipschitz in space: there exists K>0K>0K>0 such that for all t∈[0,T]t\in[0,T]t∈[0,T] and x,y∈Rx,y\in\mathbb Rx,y∈R,
∣b(t,x)−b(t,y)∣+∣σ(t,x)−σ(t,y)∣≤K∣x−y∣.|b(t,x)-b(t,y)| + |\sigma(t,x)-\sigma(t,y)| \le K|x-y|.∣b(t,x)−b(t,y)∣+∣σ(t,x)−σ(t,y)∣≤K∣x−y∣.
  1. Linear growth: there exists C>0C>0C>0 such that
∣b(t,x)∣2+∣σ(t,x)∣2≤C(1+x2).|b(t,x)|^2 + |\sigma(t,x)|^2 \le C(1+x^2).∣b(t,x)∣2+∣σ(t,x)∣2≤C(1+x2).

Under these conditions and any F0\mathcal F_0F0​-measurable X0X_0X0​ with E[X02]<∞\mathbb E[X_0^2]<\inftyE[X02​]<∞, the SDE has a unique strong solution satisfying E[sup⁡t≤TXt2]<∞\mathbb E[\sup_{t\le T}X_t^2]<\inftyE[supt≤T​Xt2​]<∞.

4.4 Picard iteration: constructing the solution​

The proof uses the same idea as the classical Picard–Lindelöf theorem for ODEs.

Define the sequence Xt(0)=X0X^{(0)}_t = X_0Xt(0)​=X0​ (constant), and

Xt(n+1)=X0+∫0tb(s,Xs(n)) ds+∫0tσ(s,Xs(n)) dWs.X^{(n+1)}_t = X_0 + \int_0^t b(s,X^{(n)}_s)\,ds + \int_0^t \sigma(s,X^{(n)}_s)\,dW_s.Xt(n+1)​=X0​+∫0t​b(s,Xs(n)​)ds+∫0t​σ(s,Xs(n)​)dWs​.

Each X(n+1)X^{(n+1)}X(n+1) is well-defined because X(n)X^{(n)}X(n) is adapted and square-integrable. We show the sequence converges.

Convergence of Picard iterates

Let Zt(n)=Xt(n+1)−Xt(n)Z^{(n)}_t = X^{(n+1)}_t - X^{(n)}_tZt(n)​=Xt(n+1)​−Xt(n)​ and εn(t)=E[sup⁡s≤t∣Zs(n)∣2]\varepsilon_n(t) = \mathbb E[\sup_{s\le t}|Z^{(n)}_s|^2]εn​(t)=E[sups≤t​∣Zs(n)​∣2].

By the Lipschitz condition and the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (Section 2.9):

εn+1(t)≤C∫0tεn(s) ds.\varepsilon_{n+1}(t) \le C\int_0^t \varepsilon_n(s)\,ds.εn+1​(t)≤C∫0t​εn​(s)ds.

Starting from ε0(t)=E[sup⁡s≤t∣Xs(1)−X0∣2]≤C0t\varepsilon_0(t) = \mathbb E[\sup_{s\le t}|X^{(1)}_s - X_0|^2] \le C_0 tε0​(t)=E[sups≤t​∣Xs(1)​−X0​∣2]≤C0​t (from linear growth), iterating:

εn(t)≤Cntnn!C0.\varepsilon_n(t) \le \frac{C^n t^n}{n!}C_0.εn​(t)≤n!Cntn​C0​.

Therefore ∑nεn(T)1/2<∞\sum_n\varepsilon_n(T)^{1/2} < \infty∑n​εn​(T)1/2<∞, and the Borel–Cantelli argument gives almost sure and L2L^2L2 convergence.

Picard iteration converging to OU solution dX = −X dt + dW
Picard iteration builds X⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾ by plugging X⁽ⁿ⁾ into the drift integral. Starting from the constant x₀, each iterate adds one more term of the exponential expansion of e^−t. Under Lipschitz conditions the iterates converge in L² — this is the constructive proof of existence.

4.5 Uniqueness via Gronwall's inequality​

Pathwise uniqueness

Under the Lipschitz condition, if XXX and YYY both solve the SDE with the same initial condition and the same Brownian motion, then Xt=YtX_t = Y_tXt​=Yt​ almost surely for all ttt.

Proof

Let Zt=Xt−YtZ_t = X_t - Y_tZt​=Xt​−Yt​. Then

Zt=∫0t[b(s,Xs)−b(s,Ys)] ds+∫0t[σ(s,Xs)−σ(s,Ys)] dWs.Z_t = \int_0^t [b(s,X_s)-b(s,Y_s)]\,ds + \int_0^t [\sigma(s,X_s)-\sigma(s,Y_s)]\,dW_s.Zt​=∫0t​[b(s,Xs​)−b(s,Ys​)]ds+∫0t​[σ(s,Xs​)−σ(s,Ys​)]dWs​.

By Lipschitz and the Itô isometry/BDG inequality:

E ⁣[sup⁡s≤tZs2]≤C∫0tE[Zs2] ds≤C∫0tE ⁣[sup⁡r≤sZr2]ds.\mathbb E\!\left[\sup_{s\le t}Z_s^2\right] \le C\int_0^t \mathbb E[Z_s^2]\,ds \le C\int_0^t \mathbb E\!\left[\sup_{r\le s}Z_r^2\right]ds.E[s≤tsup​Zs2​]≤C∫0t​E[Zs2​]ds≤C∫0t​E[r≤ssup​Zr2​]ds.

Let u(t)=E[sup⁡s≤tZs2]u(t) = \mathbb E[\sup_{s\le t}Z_s^2]u(t)=E[sups≤t​Zs2​]. Then u(t)≤C∫0tu(s) dsu(t)\le C\int_0^t u(s)\,dsu(t)≤C∫0t​u(s)ds with u(0)=0u(0)=0u(0)=0.

Gronwall's inequality: If u(t)≤A+C∫0tu(s) dsu(t)\le A + C\int_0^t u(s)\,dsu(t)≤A+C∫0t​u(s)ds with A=0A=0A=0, then u(t)≤0u(t)\le 0u(t)≤0. Therefore u≡0u\equiv 0u≡0, so Zt=0Z_t = 0Zt​=0 a.s.

Gronwall as an exponential estimate

Gronwall's inequality says: if a non-negative function grows no faster than a linear fraction of its own past accumulation, then it must be zero (when starting from zero). It is the integral analogue of the comparison principle for ODEs: if u˙≤Cu\dot u \le Cuu˙≤Cu, then u(t)≤u(0)eCtu(t)\le u(0)e^{Ct}u(t)≤u(0)eCt. When u(0)=0u(0)=0u(0)=0, u≡0u\equiv 0u≡0.

4.6 The Markov property of SDE solutions​

Under the standard conditions, the strong solution of

dXt=b(t,Xt) dt+σ(t,Xt) dWtdX_t = b(t,X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t,X_t)\,dW_tdXt​=b(t,Xt​)dt+σ(t,Xt​)dWt​

inherits the Markov property from Brownian motion: the future evolution depends on the past only through the current value XtX_tXt​.

More precisely, for s≤ts\le ts≤t and measurable fff:

E[f(Xt)∣Fs]=E[f(Xt)∣Xs].\mathbb E[f(X_t)\mid\mathcal F_s] = \mathbb E[f(X_t)\mid X_s].E[f(Xt​)∣Fs​]=E[f(Xt​)∣Xs​].

This follows from pathwise uniqueness and the fact that the increments Wt−WsW_t - W_sWt​−Ws​ are independent of Fs\mathcal F_sFs​.

4.8 A worked example: verification​

Verify that Xt=x0e(μ−σ2/2)t+σWtX_t = x_0 e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)t + \sigma W_t}Xt​=x0​e(μ−σ2/2)t+σWt​ solves dX=μX dt+σX dWdX = \mu X\,dt + \sigma X\,dWdX=μXdt+σXdW.

Apply Itô's formula to f(t,x)=x0e(μ−σ2/2)t+σxf(t,x) = x_0 e^{(\mu-\sigma^2/2)t+\sigma x}f(t,x)=x0​e(μ−σ2/2)t+σx:

∂tf=(μ−σ22)f,∂xf=σf,∂xxf=σ2f.\partial_t f = \left(\mu-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)f,\quad \partial_x f = \sigma f,\quad \partial_{xx}f = \sigma^2 f.∂t​f=(μ−2σ2​)f,∂x​f=σf,∂xx​f=σ2f.

Therefore

dXt=[(μ−σ22)f+12σ2f]dt+σf dWt=μXt dt+σXt dWt.dX_t = \left[\left(\mu-\tfrac{\sigma^2}{2}\right)f + \tfrac12\sigma^2 f\right]dt + \sigma f\,dW_t = \mu X_t\,dt + \sigma X_t\,dW_t.dXt​=[(μ−2σ2​)f+21​σ2f]dt+σfdWt​=μXt​dt+σXt​dWt​.

The SDE is satisfied. Combined with uniqueness, this is the unique strong solution.

Solution 1

∣b(x)−b(y)∣=∣x2−y2∣=∣x+y∣⋅∣x−y∣|b(x)-b(y)| = |x^2-y^2| = |x+y|\cdot|x-y|∣b(x)−b(y)∣=∣x2−y2∣=∣x+y∣⋅∣x−y∣.
