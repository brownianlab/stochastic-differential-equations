Chapter 6 — Martingales and Change of Measure

Guiding question

A drifted Brownian motion dX=θ dt+dWdX = \theta\,dt + dWdX=θdt+dW is not a martingale — it has a trend. Can we change the probability measure so that XXX becomes a martingale? If so, how does the change of measure interact with option pricing?

Girsanov's theorem says: yes, a drift can be removed by reweighting paths. The reweighting factor — the Radon–Nikodym derivative — is a stochastic exponential built from the drift itself. This is the mathematical engine behind risk-neutral pricing: the risk-neutral measure is obtained by Girsanov's theorem applied to the market price of risk.

6.1 Equivalent measures and Radon–Nikodym derivatives​

Two probability measures P\mathbb PP and Q\mathbb QQ on (Ω,F)(\Omega,\mathcal F)(Ω,F) are equivalent if they agree on which events have probability zero: P(A)=0  ⟺  Q(A)=0\mathbb P(A)=0\iff\mathbb Q(A)=0P(A)=0⟺Q(A)=0.

For equivalent measures on a finite horizon [0,T][0,T][0,T], the Radon–Nikodym theorem guarantees the existence of a density:

dQdP∣FT=ZT>0a.s.,\frac{d\mathbb Q}{d\mathbb P}\bigg|_{\mathcal F_T} = Z_T > 0\quad\text{a.s.},dPdQ​​FT​​=ZT​>0a.s.,

with EP[ZT]=1\mathbb E^{\mathbb P}[Z_T] = 1EP[ZT​]=1. For any FT\mathcal F_TFT​-measurable XXX:

EQ[X]=EP[ZT X].\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[X] = \mathbb E^{\mathbb P}[Z_T\,X].EQ[X]=EP[ZT​X].

The conditional Radon–Nikodym derivative defines a (P,Ft)(\mathbb P,\mathcal F_t)(P,Ft​)-martingale:

Zt=EP[ZT∣Ft].Z_t = \mathbb E^{\mathbb P}[Z_T\mid\mathcal F_t].Zt​=EP[ZT​∣Ft​].

6.2 The stochastic exponential​

For an adapted process θ=(θt)0≤t≤T\theta = (\theta_t)_{0\le t\le T}θ=(θt​)0≤t≤T​ satisfying the Novikov condition

EP ⁣[exp⁡ ⁣(12∫0Tθt2 dt)]<∞,\mathbb E^{\mathbb P}\!\left[\exp\!\left(\tfrac12\int_0^T\theta_t^2\,dt\right)\right] < \infty,EP[exp(21​∫0T​θt2​dt)]<∞,

define the stochastic exponential (Doléans–Dade exponential):

Zt=Et(θ):=exp⁡ ⁣(∫0tθs dWs−12∫0tθs2 ds).Z_t = \mathcal E_t(\theta) := \exp\!\left(\int_0^t\theta_s\,dW_s - \tfrac12\int_0^t\theta_s^2\,ds\right).Zt​=Et​(θ):=exp(∫0t​θs​dWs​−21​∫0t​θs2​ds).
$Z_t$ is a martingale

Under the Novikov condition, ZtZ_tZt​ is a (P,Ft)(\mathbb P,\mathcal F_t)(P,Ft​)-martingale with Z0=1Z_0 = 1Z0​=1.

SDE for $Z_t$

Apply Itô's formula to f(t,x)=exf(t,x) = e^xf(t,x)=ex where x=∫0tθs dWs−12∫0tθs2 dsx = \int_0^t\theta_s\,dW_s - \frac12\int_0^t\theta_s^2\,dsx=∫0t​θs​dWs​−21​∫0t​θs2​ds:

dZt=Zt θt dWt.dZ_t = Z_t\,\theta_t\,dW_t.dZt​=Zt​θt​dWt​.

The drift vanishes — ZtZ_tZt​ satisfies an SDE with zero drift coefficient. Under the Novikov condition, ZtZ_tZt​ is a true L2L^2L2-martingale (not merely a local martingale).

6.3 Girsanov's theorem​

Girsanov's theorem

Let θ=(θt)\theta = (\theta_t)θ=(θt​) be adapted and satisfy the Novikov condition. Define the measure Q\mathbb QQ by

dQdP∣FT=ZT=exp⁡ ⁣(∫0Tθt dWt−12∫0Tθt2 dt).\frac{d\mathbb Q}{d\mathbb P}\bigg|_{\mathcal F_T} = Z_T = \exp\!\left(\int_0^T\theta_t\,dW_t - \tfrac12\int_0^T\theta_t^2\,dt\right).dPdQ​​FT​​=ZT​=exp(∫0T​θt​dWt​−21​∫0T​θt2​dt).

Then Q\mathbb QQ is a probability measure equivalent to P\mathbb PP, and the process

W~t=Wt−∫0tθs ds\widetilde W_t = W_t - \int_0^t\theta_s\,dsWt​=Wt​−∫0t​θs​ds

is a standard Brownian motion under Q\mathbb QQ.

Proof sketch

By Lévy's characterization theorem, it suffices to show that W~t\widetilde W_tWt​ is a Q\mathbb QQ-martingale with quadratic variation [W~]t=t[\widetilde W]_t = t[W]t​=t.

Quadratic variation. dW~t=dWt−θt dtd\widetilde W_t = dW_t - \theta_t\,dtdWt​=dWt​−θt​dt, so (dW~t)2=(dWt)2=dt(d\widetilde W_t)^2 = (dW_t)^2 = dt(dWt​)2=(dWt​)2=dt. The quadratic variation is unchanged by Girsanov.

Martingale. For s≤ts\le ts≤t, using Bayes' rule under Q\mathbb QQ:

EQ[W~t∣Fs]=EP[ZtW~t∣Fs]Zs.\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[\widetilde W_t\mid\mathcal F_s] = \frac{\mathbb E^{\mathbb P}[Z_t\widetilde W_t\mid\mathcal F_s]}{Z_s}.EQ[Wt​∣Fs​]=Zs​EP[Zt​Wt​∣Fs​]​.

Write W~t=W~s+∫stdW~r\widetilde W_t = \widetilde W_s + \int_s^t d\widetilde W_rWt​=Ws​+∫st​dWr​. An Itô computation shows ZtW~t−ZsW~s=∫st(⋯ ) dWrZ_t\widetilde W_t - Z_s\widetilde W_s = \int_s^t(\cdots)\,dW_rZt​Wt​−Zs​Ws​=∫st​(⋯)dWr​ (pure stochastic integral), so EP[ZtW~t−ZsW~s∣Fs]=0\mathbb E^{\mathbb P}[Z_t\widetilde W_t-Z_s\widetilde W_s\mid\mathcal F_s]=0EP[Zt​Wt​−Zs​Ws​∣Fs​]=0, giving EQ[W~t∣Fs]=W~s\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[\widetilde W_t\mid\mathcal F_s] = \widetilde W_sEQ[Wt​∣Fs​]=Ws​.

6.4 The key formula: drift and measure​

Under P\mathbb PP, the process Xt=∫0tθs ds+WtX_t = \int_0^t\theta_s\,ds + W_tXt​=∫0t​θs​ds+Wt​ has drift θ\thetaθ.

Under Q\mathbb QQ (with W~=W−∫θ ds\widetilde W = W - \int\theta\,dsW=W−∫θds), we can write Xt=∫0tθs ds+Wt=W~t+2∫0tθs dsX_t = \int_0^t\theta_s\,ds + W_t = \widetilde W_t + 2\int_0^t\theta_s\,dsXt​=∫0t​θs​ds+Wt​=Wt​+2∫0t​θs​ds. Wait — let's redo this cleanly.

Under P\mathbb PP: WtW_tWt​ is BM, so Xt=∫0tθs ds+WtX_t = \int_0^t\theta_s\,ds + W_tXt​=∫0t​θs​ds+Wt​ has drift θt\theta_tθt​.

Under Q\mathbb QQ: W~t=Wt−∫0tθs ds\widetilde W_t = W_t - \int_0^t\theta_s\,dsWt​=Wt​−∫0t​θs​ds is BM, so Wt=W~t+∫0tθs dsW_t = \widetilde W_t + \int_0^t\theta_s\,dsWt​=Wt​+∫0t​θs​ds, meaning Xt=W~tX_t = \widetilde W_tXt​=Wt​: the drift is gone. Girsanov removes the drift.

More generally, if dXt=bt dt+dWtdX_t = b_t\,dt + dW_tdXt​=bt​dt+dWt​ under P\mathbb PP, then under Q\mathbb QQ (with θt=−bt\theta_t = -b_tθt​=−bt​):

dXt=dW~t,dX_t = d\widetilde W_t,dXt​=dWt​,

so XXX becomes pure BM under Q\mathbb QQ.

Drift as the geometry of measure change

A drift θt\theta_tθt​ tilts the probability measure in path space: paths going upward become relatively more likely under P\mathbb PP than under the baseline BM. Girsanov's theorem reverses this tilt: ZTZ_TZT​ reweights each path by a factor that exactly compensates the drift.

The stochastic exponential ZT=e∫θ dW−12∫θ2 dtZ_T = e^{\int\theta\,dW - \frac12\int\theta^2\,dt}ZT​=e∫θdW−21​∫θ2dt is large for paths that move against the drift (providing extra weight) and small for paths that move with the drift (reducing their weight).

6.5 Martingale representation theorem​

Martingale representation (Clark–Ocone)

Let (Mt)0≤t≤T(M_t)_{0\le t\le T}(Mt​)0≤t≤T​ be any square-integrable (Ft)(\mathcal F_t)(Ft​)-martingale (where Ft=σ(Ws:s≤t)\mathcal F_t = \sigma(W_s: s\le t)Ft​=σ(Ws​:s≤t)). Then there exists a unique adapted process H∈L2H\in\mathcal L^2H∈L2 such that

Mt=M0+∫0tHs dWs.M_t = M_0 + \int_0^t H_s\,dW_s.Mt​=M0​+∫0t​Hs​dWs​.

This theorem says that every martingale in the Brownian filtration is an Itô integral. There are no other sources of randomness — Brownian motion is the only martingale "atom."

Completeness and perfect hedging

In a model driven by a single Brownian motion, the martingale representation theorem implies that every contingent claim can be perfectly replicated by a dynamic trading strategy in the underlying asset. The replication is exactly the stochastic integral ∫Ht dSt\int H_t\,dS_t∫Ht​dSt​, where HtH_tHt​ is the hedge ratio.

Markets with ddd independent Brownian motions and ddd tradable assets are complete: every claim is replicable. Incompleteness (stochastic volatility, jumps) introduces additional sources of randomness not spanned by the tradable assets.

6.6 Finance: the risk-neutral measure​

In the Black–Scholes model:

dSt=μSt dt+σSt dWtP,dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t^{\mathbb P},dSt​=μSt​dt+σSt​dWtP​,

the discounted stock price S~t=e−rtSt\tilde S_t = e^{-rt}S_tS~t​=e−rtSt​ satisfies

dS~t=(μ−r)S~t dt+σS~t dWtP.d\tilde S_t = (\mu-r)\tilde S_t\,dt + \sigma\tilde S_t\,dW_t^{\mathbb P}.dS~t​=(μ−r)S~t​dt+σS~t​dWtP​.

For S~t\tilde S_tS~t​ to be a martingale (as required by no-arbitrage), the drift must vanish. Apply Girsanov with θt=−μ−rσ\theta_t = -\frac{\mu-r}{\sigma}θt​=−σμ−r​ (the market price of risk):

W~t=WtP+μ−rσt\widetilde W_t = W_t^{\mathbb P} + \frac{\mu-r}{\sigma}tWt​=WtP​+σμ−r​t

is a Q\mathbb QQ-Brownian motion. Under Q\mathbb QQ:

dSt=rSt dt+σSt dW~t.dS_t = r S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,d\widetilde W_t.dSt​=rSt​dt+σSt​dWt​.

The drift equals the risk-free rate: the stock earns rrr under Q\mathbb QQ.

Risk-neutral pricing formula

Under the risk-neutral measure Q\mathbb QQ, the price of any replicable claim with payoff g(ST)g(S_T)g(ST​) is

Vt=e−r(T−t)EQ[g(ST)∣Ft].V_t = e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[g(S_T)\mid\mathcal F_t].Vt​=e−r(T−t)EQ[g(ST​)∣Ft​].

The measure Q\mathbb QQ is called risk-neutral because under it all assets earn the risk-free rate — investors are indifferent to risk. The real-world drift μ\muμ disappears from the pricing formula. This is why you cannot infer μ\muμ from option prices.

Under P\mathbb PP, let dX=−κX dt+dWdX = -\kappa X\,dt + dWdX=−κXdt+dW (OU toward zero). Find the measure Q\mathbb QQ under which XXX is a pure Brownian motion. Write down the Girsanov density dQ/dPdQ/dPdQ/dP.

Solution 1

E[ZT]=E[eθWT−θ2T/2]\mathbb E[Z_T] = \mathbb E[e^{\theta W_T - \theta^2 T/2}]E[ZT​]=E[eθWT​−θ2T/2]. Since WT∼N(0,T)W_T\sim N(0,T)WT​∼N(0,T):

E[eθWT]=eθ2T/2,\mathbb E[e^{\theta W_T}] = e^{\theta^2 T/2},E[eθWT​]=eθ2T/2,

so E[ZT]=eθ2T/2⋅e−θ2T/2=1\mathbb E[Z_T] = e^{\theta^2 T/2}\cdot e^{-\theta^2 T/2} = 1E[ZT​]=eθ2T/2⋅e−θ2T/2=1. For E[ZT2]=E[e2θWT−θ2T]=e2θ2T⋅e−θ2T=eθ2T\mathbb E[Z_T^2] = \mathbb E[e^{2\theta W_T - \theta^2 T}] = e^{2\theta^2 T}\cdot e^{-\theta^2 T} = e^{\theta^2 T}E[ZT2​]=E[e2θWT​−θ2T]=e2θ2T⋅e−θ2T=eθ2T.

Solution 2

The OU drift is bt=−κXtb_t = -\kappa X_tbt​=−κXt​. To remove this drift via Girsanov, set θt=κXt\theta_t = \kappa X_tθt​=κXt​ (the market price of risk equals the negative of the drift). Under Q\mathbb QQ with Radon–Nikodym derivative

dQdP=exp⁡ ⁣(κ∫0TXt dWt−12κ2∫0TXt2 dt),\frac{d\mathbb Q}{d\mathbb P} = \exp\!\left(\kappa\int_0^T X_t\,dW_t - \tfrac12\kappa^2\int_0^T X_t^2\,dt\right),dPdQ​=exp(κ∫0T​Xt​dWt​−21​κ2∫0T​Xt2​dt),

the process W~t=Wt−κ∫0tXs ds\widetilde W_t = W_t - \kappa\int_0^t X_s\,dsWt​=Wt​−κ∫0t​Xs​ds is a Q\mathbb QQ-Brownian motion, and dX=dW~dX = d\widetilde WdX=dW, making XXX pure BM.
