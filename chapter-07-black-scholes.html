Chapter 7 — Black–Scholes from First Principles

Guiding question

Given a stock following dS=μS dt+σS dWdS = \mu S\,dt + \sigma S\,dWdS=μSdt+σSdW, what is the fair price of an option on SSS? The answer should depend only on observable quantities, not on μ\muμ — the investor's subjective view of expected return. How can this be, and how do we derive the formula rigorously?

Black–Scholes is the synthesis of everything built in Chapters 1–6. It requires:

  • GBM as the stock model (Chapter 3, 5)
  • Itô's formula to compute option price dynamics (Chapter 3)
  • Self-financing portfolio and replication (new concept here)
  • The PDE or risk-neutral pricing formula (Chapters 6, 10)

7.1 The model setup​

Stock: dSt=μSt dt+σSt dWtPdS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t^{\mathbb P}dSt​=μSt​dt+σSt​dWtP​, with S0>0S_0 > 0S0​>0, σ>0\sigma > 0σ>0.

Bond: dBt=rBt dtdB_t = r B_t\,dtdBt​=rBt​dt (risk-free savings account), so Bt=B0ertB_t = B_0 e^{rt}Bt​=B0​ert.

Option: a European call with payoff g(ST)=(ST−K)+g(S_T) = (S_T - K)^+g(ST​)=(ST​−K)+ at time TTT.

Objective: find the replicating portfolio and its cost at time 000.

7.2 The self-financing condition​

A portfolio (Δt,ψt)(\Delta_t, \psi_t)(Δt​,ψt​) consists of Δt\Delta_tΔt​ shares of stock and ψt\psi_tψt​ units of bond. Its value is:

Vt=ΔtSt+ψtBt.V_t = \Delta_t S_t + \psi_t B_t.Vt​=Δt​St​+ψt​Bt​.

The portfolio is self-financing if all changes in value come from capital gains — no external cash flows:

dVt=Δt dSt+ψt dBt.dV_t = \Delta_t\,dS_t + \psi_t\,dB_t.dVt​=Δt​dSt​+ψt​dBt​.

In words: the change in wealth equals the gain from the stock position plus the gain from the bond position, with no injections or withdrawals.

Using dSt=μSt dt+σSt dWtdS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_tdSt​=μSt​dt+σSt​dWt​ and dBt=rBt dtdB_t = rB_t\,dtdBt​=rBt​dt:

dVt=Δt(μSt dt+σSt dWt)+ψtrBt dt=(μΔtSt+rψtBt) dt+σΔtSt dWt.dV_t = \Delta_t(\mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t) + \psi_t rB_t\,dt = (\mu\Delta_t S_t + r\psi_t B_t)\,dt + \sigma\Delta_t S_t\,dW_t.dVt​=Δt​(μSt​dt+σSt​dWt​)+ψt​rBt​dt=(μΔt​St​+rψt​Bt​)dt+σΔt​St​dWt​.

7.3 The replication argument​

Assume the option price has the form Vt=f(t,St)V_t = f(t,S_t)Vt​=f(t,St​) for some smooth function fff. Apply Itô's formula:

dVt=df(t,St)=[∂tf+μSt∂Sf+12σ2St2∂SSf]dt+σSt∂Sf dWt.dV_t = df(t,S_t) = \left[\partial_t f + \mu S_t\partial_S f + \tfrac12\sigma^2 S_t^2\partial_{SS}f\right]dt + \sigma S_t\partial_S f\,dW_t.dVt​=df(t,St​)=[∂t​f+μSt​∂S​f+21​σ2St2​∂SS​f]dt+σSt​∂S​fdWt​.

Matching the dWtdW_tdWt​ term with the self-financing condition:

σSt∂Sf=σΔtSt  ⟹  Δt=∂Sf(t,St).\sigma S_t\partial_S f = \sigma\Delta_t S_t \implies \boxed{\Delta_t = \partial_S f(t,S_t).}σSt​∂S​f=σΔt​St​⟹Δt​=∂S​f(t,St​).​

The hedge ratio is Δ=∂Sf\Delta = \partial_S fΔ=∂S​f, the delta of the option.

Matching the dtdtdt terms:

∂tf+μS∂Sf+12σ2S2∂SSf=μΔS+rψB.\partial_t f + \mu S\partial_S f + \tfrac12\sigma^2 S^2\partial_{SS}f = \mu\Delta S + r\psi B.∂t​f+μS∂S​f+21​σ2S2∂SS​f=μΔS+rψB.

Substituting Δ=∂Sf\Delta = \partial_S fΔ=∂S​f and V=f=ΔS+ψBV = f = \Delta S + \psi BV=f=ΔS+ψB, so ψB=f−ΔS=f−S∂Sf\psi B = f - \Delta S = f - S\partial_S fψB=f−ΔS=f−S∂S​f:

∂tf+μS∂Sf+12σ2S2∂SSf=μS∂Sf+r(f−S∂Sf).\partial_t f + \mu S\partial_S f + \tfrac12\sigma^2 S^2\partial_{SS}f = \mu S\partial_S f + r(f - S\partial_S f).∂t​f+μS∂S​f+21​σ2S2∂SS​f=μS∂S​f+r(f−S∂S​f).

The μ\muμ terms cancel:

∂tf+rS ∂Sf+12σ2S2 ∂SSf−rf=0.\boxed{\partial_t f + rS\,\partial_S f + \tfrac12\sigma^2 S^2\,\partial_{SS}f - rf = 0.}∂t​f+rS∂S​f+21​σ2S2∂SS​f−rf=0.​

This is the Black–Scholes PDE.

Why $\mu$ disappears

The hedge Δ=∂Sf\Delta = \partial_S fΔ=∂S​f exactly cancels the stochastic term. The remaining PDE has no μ\muμ — the investor's subjective expected return is irrelevant. This is the mathematical statement of no-arbitrage: if two investors with different μ\muμ beliefs both delta-hedge, they hold the same portfolio and must agree on the option price.

Economically, delta-hedging eliminates the risk of the position. The hedged portfolio earns the risk-free rate rrr, regardless of μ\muμ.

7.4 Boundary conditions and the solution​

The PDE is solved on (t,S)∈[0,T)×(0,∞)(t,S)\in[0,T)\times(0,\infty)(t,S)∈[0,T)×(0,∞) with terminal condition

f(T,S)=(S−K)+.f(T,S) = (S-K)^+.f(T,S)=(S−K)+.

For S→0S\to 0S→0: f→0f\to 0f→0. For S→∞S\to\inftyS→∞: f∼S−Ke−r(T−t)f\sim S - Ke^{-r(T-t)}f∼S−Ke−r(T−t) (deep in-the-money).

The change of variables x=log⁡Sx = \log Sx=logS transforms the PDE to the heat equation. The solution is:

Black–Scholes formula

The price of a European call option is

C(t,S)=S N(d1)−Ke−r(T−t) N(d2),C(t,S) = S\,N(d_1) - Ke^{-r(T-t)}\,N(d_2),C(t,S)=SN(d1​)−Ke−r(T−t)N(d2​),

where

d1=log⁡(S/K)+(r+σ2/2)(T−t)σT−t,d2=d1−σT−t,d_1 = \frac{\log(S/K) + (r+\sigma^2/2)(T-t)}{\sigma\sqrt{T-t}}, \qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T-t},d1​=σT−t​log(S/K)+(r+σ2/2)(T−t)​,d2​=d1​−σT−t​,

and N(⋅)N(\cdot)N(⋅) is the standard normal CDF.

Derivation via heat equation

Let τ=T−t\tau = T-tτ=T−t (time to maturity) and x=log⁡(S/K)+(r−σ2/2)τx = \log(S/K) + (r-\sigma^2/2)\taux=log(S/K)+(r−σ2/2)τ. The Black–Scholes PDE becomes the heat equation ∂τu=σ22∂xxu\partial_\tau u = \frac{\sigma^2}{2}\partial_{xx}u∂τ​u=2σ2​∂xx​u with initial condition u(0,x)=K(ex−1)+u(0,x) = K(e^x - 1)^+u(0,x)=K(ex−1)+.

The solution is given by convolution with the Gaussian kernel:

u(τ,x)=1σ2πτ∫−∞∞K(ey−1)+e−(x−y)2/(2σ2τ) dy.u(\tau,x) = \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi\tau}}\int_{-\infty}^\infty K(e^y-1)^+ e^{-(x-y)^2/(2\sigma^2\tau)}\,dy.u(τ,x)=σ2πτ​1​∫−∞∞​K(ey−1)+e−(x−y)2/(2σ2τ)dy.

Evaluating this integral (split at y=0y=0y=0) yields the Black–Scholes formula.

7.5 The Greeks​

The Greeks are sensitivities of the option price to model parameters:

Δ=∂SC=N(d1),Γ=∂SSC=N′(d1)Sστ,\Delta = \partial_S C = N(d_1), \qquad \Gamma = \partial_{SS} C = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S\sigma\sqrt{\tau}},Δ=∂S​C=N(d1​),Γ=∂SS​C=Sστ​N′(d1​)​, Θ=∂tC=−SN′(d1)σ2τ−rKe−rτN(d2),V=∂σC=SN′(d1)τ.\Theta = \partial_t C = -\frac{SN'(d_1)\sigma}{2\sqrt{\tau}} - rKe^{-r\tau}N(d_2), \qquad \mathcal V = \partial_\sigma C = SN'(d_1)\sqrt{\tau}.Θ=∂t​C=−2τ​SN′(d1​)σ​−rKe−rτN(d2​),V=∂σ​C=SN′(d1​)τ​.

The Black–Scholes PDE can be written compactly as:

Θ+rSΔ+12σ2S2Γ=rC.\Theta + rS\Delta + \tfrac12\sigma^2 S^2\Gamma = rC.Θ+rSΔ+21​σ2S2Γ=rC.

This says: time decay plus drift income plus convexity income equals the risk-free return on the option value.

Gamma is the curvature cost

Γ=∂SSC>0\Gamma = \partial_{SS}C > 0Γ=∂SS​C>0 means the option is a convex function of SSS. By Itô's formula, (dS)2=σ2S2 dt(dS)^2 = \sigma^2 S^2\,dt(dS)2=σ2S2dt generates a contribution 12Γσ2S2 dt\frac12\Gamma\sigma^2 S^2\,dt21​Γσ2S2dt to the instantaneous P&L of the delta-hedged portfolio. This convexity income is what the option holder "earns" by gamma scalping — rebalancing the delta hedge as SSS moves. It is exactly balanced by time decay Θ\ThetaΘ.

7.6 Put–call parity​

By no-arbitrage, the put price P(t,S)=Ke−rτN(−d2)−SN(−d1)P(t,S) = Ke^{-r\tau}N(-d_2) - SN(-d_1)P(t,S)=Ke−rτN(−d2​)−SN(−d1​) satisfies:

C−P=S−Ke−rτ.C - P = S - Ke^{-r\tau}.C−P=S−Ke−rτ.

This is put–call parity and requires no model assumptions — only the absence of arbitrage and the definitions of the payoffs.

7.7 The risk-neutral derivation​

Alternatively, by Chapter 6, under Q\mathbb QQ:

dS=rS dt+σS dW~,ST=Stexp⁡ ⁣[(r−12σ2)(T−t)+σ(W~T−W~t)].dS = rS\,dt + \sigma S\,d\widetilde W, \qquad S_T = S_t\exp\!\left[(r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)(T-t) + \sigma(\widetilde W_T - \widetilde W_t)\right].dS=rSdt+σSdW,ST​=St​exp[(r−21​σ2)(T−t)+σ(WT​−Wt​)].

The call price is:

C(t,S)=e−rτEQ ⁣[(ST−K)+∣St=S].C(t,S) = e^{-r\tau}\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}\!\left[(S_T-K)^+\mid S_t = S\right].C(t,S)=e−rτEQ[(ST​−K)+∣St​=S].

Since log⁡(ST/S)∼N((r−σ2/2)τ,σ2τ)\log(S_T/S)\sim N((r-\sigma^2/2)\tau, \sigma^2\tau)log(ST​/S)∼N((r−σ2/2)τ,σ2τ) under Q\mathbb QQ, the expectation evaluates to the Black–Scholes formula. This derivation makes explicit that CCC is an expectation — it can be computed by Monte Carlo simulation.
