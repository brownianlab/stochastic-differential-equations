Chapter 12 — Interest Rate Models

Guiding question

Modeling equity prices requires one SDE. Modeling the yield curve requires modeling infinitely many correlated rates simultaneously. How do we build self-consistent models for the entire term structure that remain arbitrage-free?

Interest rate modeling is fundamentally different from equity modeling: the "underlying" is not one number but a curve — the function T↦r(t,T)T\mapsto r(t,T)T↦r(t,T) mapping maturity to yield. Every point on this curve is a derivative of the short rate rt=r(t,t)r_t = r(t,t)rt​=r(t,t), and all must move consistently under one probability measure.

12.3 The CIR model​

To avoid negative rates, Cox–Ingersoll–Ross proposed:

drt=κ(θ−rt) dt+σrt dWt.dr_t = \kappa(\theta-r_t)\,dt + \sigma\sqrt{r_t}\,dW_t.drt​=κ(θ−rt​)dt+σrt​​dWt​.

12.4 Affine term structure models​

Both Vasicek and CIR are special cases of affine term structure models (ATSMs). An ATSM assumes the state vector Xt\mathbf X_tXt​ follows an affine diffusion:

dXt=(κθ−KXt) dt+Σ(Xt) dWt,d\mathbf X_t = (\boldsymbol\kappa\boldsymbol\theta - K\mathbf X_t)\,dt + \Sigma(\mathbf X_t)\,d\mathbf W_t,dXt​=(κθ−KXt​)dt+Σ(Xt​)dWt​,

with the short rate rt=δ0+δ⋅Xtr_t = \delta_0 + \boldsymbol\delta\cdot\mathbf X_trt​=δ0​+δ⋅Xt​ (affine in the state).

Key result: bond prices are exponential-affine: P(t,T)=eA(τ)+B(τ)⋅XtP(t,T) = e^{A(\tau)+\mathbf B(\tau)\cdot\mathbf X_t}P(t,T)=eA(τ)+B(τ)⋅Xt​, where (A,B)(A,\mathbf B)(A,B) solve a system of Riccati ODEs.

The An(m)A_n(m)An​(m) classification (Dai–Singleton) organizes ATSMs by the number of state variables nnn and the number of correlated factors driving volatility mmm.

12.5 The HJM framework​

The Heath–Jarrow–Morton framework models the entire forward rate curve:

df(t,T)=μ(t,T) dt+σ(t,T)⋅dWt.df(t,T) = \mu(t,T)\,dt + \boldsymbol\sigma(t,T)\cdot d\mathbf W_t.df(t,T)=μ(t,T)dt+σ(t,T)⋅dWt​.

Here f(t,T)f(t,T)f(t,T) is the instantaneous forward rate for time TTT, observed at time ttt.

HJM drift condition (no-arbitrage)

Under the risk-neutral measure Q\mathbb QQ, the drift of f(t,T)f(t,T)f(t,T) is completely determined by its volatility:

μQ(t,T)=σ(t,T)⋅∫tTσ(t,s) ds.\mu^{\mathbb Q}(t,T) = \boldsymbol\sigma(t,T)\cdot\int_t^T\boldsymbol\sigma(t,s)\,ds.μQ(t,T)=σ(t,T)⋅∫tT​σ(t,s)ds.

This is the HJM drift restriction.

Proof sketch

The bond price is P(t,T)=exp⁡(−∫tTf(t,s) ds)P(t,T) = \exp(-\int_t^T f(t,s)\,ds)P(t,T)=exp(−∫tT​f(t,s)ds). For P~(t,T)=e−rtP(t,T)\tilde P(t,T) = e^{-rt}P(t,T)P~(t,T)=e−rtP(t,T) to be a Q\mathbb QQ-martingale, Itô's formula for P(t,T)P(t,T)P(t,T) (using Itô's formula for the exponential of a stochastic integral) forces the drift-volatility relationship above.

HJM as geometry of forward rates

The HJM condition says: in a no-arbitrage model, you cannot freely specify both the drift and the volatility of forward rates. Once you commit to a volatility structure σ(t,T)\sigma(t,T)σ(t,T), the drift is determined. The entire arbitrage-free family of term structure models is parameterized by the volatility structure alone.

Vasicek is HJM with σ(t,T)=σe−κ(T−t)\sigma(t,T) = \sigma e^{-\kappa(T-t)}σ(t,T)=σe−κ(T−t) (exponentially decaying vols). CIR is HJM with state-dependent vol. The HJM framework unifies all these as special cases.

12.6 The LIBOR market model​

For cap/floor pricing, the natural objects are LIBOR forward rates L(t,Ti,Ti+1)L(t,T_i,T_{i+1})L(t,Ti​,Ti+1​) for a tenor grid T0<T1<⋯<TnT_0 < T_1 < \cdots < T_nT0​<T1​<⋯<Tn​.

In the Brace–Gatarek–Musiela (BGM)/LIBOR market model, each LIBOR rate follows a lognormal process under its own forward measure (the Ti+1T_{i+1}Ti+1​-forward measure):

dLi(t)=μi(t) dt+σi(t) Li(t) dW~tTi+1,dL_i(t) = \mu_i(t)\,dt + \sigma_i(t)\,L_i(t)\,d\tilde W_t^{T_{i+1}},dLi​(t)=μi​(t)dt+σi​(t)Li​(t)dW~tTi+1​​,

where the drift μi\mu_iμi​ (under a common measure) is determined by the terminal correlation structure and the tenor basis.

This model is consistent with Black's formula for caps and floors, making calibration straightforward.

Negative rates and extensions

The Vasicek model allows negative rates, which was academic curiosity until 2014 when European central banks introduced negative policy rates. Gaussian models (Vasicek, Hull-White) became acceptable. The normal SABR model and shifted lognormal models are now standard for low-rate environments.

For a Vasicek model extended to fit the initial term structure (Hull–White extension): dr=(θ(t)−κr) dt+σ dWdr = (\theta(t) - \kappa r)\,dt + \sigma\,dWdr=(θ(t)−κr)dt+σdW, where θ(t)\theta(t)θ(t) is calibrated to match P(0,T)P(0,T)P(0,T) for all TTT. This makes the model exact for today's curve while retaining mean-reverting dynamics.
