Chapter 14 — Advanced Geometry of SDEs

Guiding question

Itô's integral has a non-zero correction term that breaks the classical chain rule. Is this a defect, or does it reveal something geometric? What happens when the state space is a manifold rather than Rn\mathbb R^nRn? And how does the theory extend when paths are rougher than Brownian?

This final chapter steps back from computation and asks: what is the intrinsic geometry of stochastic differential equations? Three interconnected ideas:

  1. Stratonovich vs Itô: the two integrals encode two different conventions about "time and space," and the Stratonovich integral is the one that respects the geometry of the state space.
  2. SDEs on manifolds: when the state space is curved (a Lie group, a sphere), the Itô and Stratonovich equations differ in a geometrically meaningful way.
  3. Rough paths: Lyon's theory extends stochastic calculus beyond semimartingales to paths of finite ppp-variation, with Brownian motion as a special case.

14.1 Revisiting the Stratonovich integral​

Recall from Chapter 2 that the Itô integral uses the left endpoint rule and the Stratonovich integral uses the midpoint rule:

∫0THt∘ dWt=lim⁡∣Π∣→0∑kHtk+Htk+12(Wtk+1−Wtk).\int_0^T H_t\circ\,dW_t = \lim_{|\Pi|\to 0}\sum_k\frac{H_{t_k}+H_{t_{k+1}}}{2}(W_{t_{k+1}}-W_{t_k}).∫0T​Ht​∘dWt​=∣Π∣→0lim​k∑​2Htk​​+Htk+1​​​(Wtk+1​​−Wtk​​).

The relationship between the two is:

∫0THt∘ dWt=∫0THt dWt+12[H,W]T.\int_0^T H_t\circ\,dW_t = \int_0^T H_t\,dW_t + \tfrac12[H,W]_T.∫0T​Ht​∘dWt​=∫0T​Ht​dWt​+21​[H,W]T​.

For Ht=f(Wt)H_t = f(W_t)Ht​=f(Wt​): [H,W]T=∫0Tf′(Wt) dt[H,W]_T = \int_0^T f'(W_t)\,dt[H,W]T​=∫0T​f′(Wt​)dt. Therefore:

∫0Tf(Wt)∘ dWt=∫0Tf(Wt) dWt+12∫0Tf′(Wt) dt.\int_0^T f(W_t)\circ\,dW_t = \int_0^T f(W_t)\,dW_t + \tfrac12\int_0^T f'(W_t)\,dt.∫0T​f(Wt​)∘dWt​=∫0T​f(Wt​)dWt​+21​∫0T​f′(Wt​)dt.

Stratonovich chain rule. For the Stratonovich integral, the chain rule is classical:

f(WT)−f(W0)=∫0Tf′(Wt)∘ dWt.f(W_T) - f(W_0) = \int_0^T f'(W_t)\circ\,dW_t.f(WT​)−f(W0​)=∫0T​f′(Wt​)∘dWt​.

No second derivative term appears. This is the "coordinate-free" form of the chain rule, which is why Stratonovich appears naturally on manifolds.

14.2 SDE conversions: Itô ↔\leftrightarrow↔ Stratonovich​

An SDE in Stratonovich form:

dXt=b(Xt) dt+σ(Xt)∘ dWtdX_t = b(X_t)\,dt + \sigma(X_t)\circ\,dW_tdXt​=b(Xt​)dt+σ(Xt​)∘dWt​

is equivalent to the Itô SDE:

dXt=[b(Xt)+12σ′(Xt)σ(Xt)]dt+σ(Xt) dWt.dX_t = \left[b(X_t) + \tfrac12\sigma'(X_t)\sigma(X_t)\right]dt + \sigma(X_t)\,dW_t.dXt​=[b(Xt​)+21​σ′(Xt​)σ(Xt​)]dt+σ(Xt​)dWt​.

The extra term 12σ′σ\frac12\sigma'\sigma21​σ′σ is the Itô–Stratonovich correction.

Why Stratonovich is coordinate-free

Under a smooth change of variables y=ϕ(x)y = \phi(x)y=ϕ(x), the Itô SDE for Yt=ϕ(Xt)Y_t = \phi(X_t)Yt​=ϕ(Xt​) involves ϕ′′\phi''ϕ′′ (from the second-order Taylor expansion). But in Stratonovich form, YtY_tYt​ satisfies the "obvious" chain rule dY=ϕ′(X)∘dXdY = \phi'(X)\circ dXdY=ϕ′(X)∘dX — no second-derivative correction. This is why Stratonovich is the correct integral for SDEs defined on manifolds (where coordinates can be changed freely) and for physical models that arise as limits of smooth systems under fast noise.

14.3 SDEs on manifolds​

Let M\mathcal MM be a smooth ddd-dimensional manifold embedded in Rn\mathbb R^nRn. A process Xt∈MX_t\in\mathcal MXt​∈M for all ttt is called an M\mathcal MM-valued diffusion.

The intrinsic SDE on M\mathcal MM is written in Stratonovich form:

dXt=V0(Xt) dt+∑k=1mVk(Xt)∘ dWtk,dX_t = V_0(X_t)\,dt + \sum_{k=1}^m V_k(X_t)\circ\,dW_t^k,dXt​=V0​(Xt​)dt+k=1∑m​Vk​(Xt​)∘dWtk​,

where V0,V1,…,VmV_0, V_1,\ldots,V_mV0​,V1​,…,Vm​ are smooth vector fields on M\mathcal MM. In local coordinates (x1,…,xd)(x^1,\ldots,x^d)(x1,…,xd):

dXti=V0i(Xt) dt+∑kVki(Xt)∘ dWtk.dX_t^i = V_0^i(X_t)\,dt + \sum_k V_k^i(X_t)\circ\,dW_t^k.dXti​=V0i​(Xt​)dt+k∑​Vki​(Xt​)∘dWtk​.

The Stratonovich form guarantees that XtX_tXt​ stays on M\mathcal MM: if ϕ:M→R\phi:\mathcal M\to\mathbb Rϕ:M→R is a smooth function, ϕ(Xt)\phi(X_t)ϕ(Xt​) satisfies the classical chain rule in Stratonovich form.

Example: Brownian motion on S2S^2S2. The 2-sphere S2={x∈R3:∣x∣=1}S^2 = \{x\in\mathbb R^3: |x|=1\}S2={x∈R3:∣x∣=1} supports a natural Brownian motion (the spherical BM). It is the Stratonovich SDE driven by the two angular Brownian motions in the tangent bundle.

Example: Stochastic flows. The stochastic flow ϕs,t:x↦Xts,x\phi_{s,t}: x\mapsto X_t^{s,x}ϕs,t​:x↦Xts,x​ (solving dX=b(X) dt+σ(X)∘dWdX = b(X)\,dt + \sigma(X)\circ dWdX=b(X)dt+σ(X)∘dW, Xs=xX_s = xXs​=x) is a family of diffeomorphisms of M\mathcal MM, driven by the Stratonovich integral.

14.4 The generator on manifolds​

In local coordinates on M\mathcal MM, the generator of the Stratonovich SDE above is:

L=V0+12∑kVk2,\mathcal L = V_0 + \tfrac12\sum_k V_k^2,L=V0​+21​k∑​Vk2​,

where Vk2=Vk(Vkf)V_k^2 = V_k(V_k f)Vk2​=Vk​(Vk​f) is the second-order differential operator. In terms of the Riemannian metric ggg on M\mathcal MM, if σσ⊤=g−1\sigma\sigma^\top = g^{-1}σσ⊤=g−1 (the inverse metric), then ∑kVk2=Δg\sum_k V_k^2 = \Delta_g∑k​Vk2​=Δg​, the Laplace–Beltrami operator.

The Brownian motion on a Riemannian manifold is defined as the diffusion with generator 12Δg\frac12\Delta_g21​Δg​.

The Laplacian as a generator

For flat space M=Rn\mathcal M = \mathbb R^nM=Rn with Euclidean metric, Δg=Δ\Delta_g = \DeltaΔg​=Δ (the ordinary Laplacian). For a sphere S2S^2S2 with the round metric, Δg\Delta_gΔg​ is the spherical Laplacian. For a financial manifold (e.g., the space of covariance matrices), the Laplacian encodes the "isotropic" diffusion intrinsic to that space.

The deep geometric fact: Brownian motion on any Riemannian manifold is the unique diffusion that is symmetric with respect to the volume measure, has no drift, and generates the Laplace–Beltrami operator. It is the canonical random walker on curved spaces.

14.5 Hörmander's theorem and hypoellipticity​

For the SDE dX=V0 dt+∑kVk∘dWkdX = V_0\,dt + \sum_k V_k\circ dW^kdX=V0​dt+∑k​Vk​∘dWk on Rn\mathbb R^nRn, the process XtX_tXt​ has a smooth density at time t>0t>0t>0 if the Lie algebra generated by V1,…,VmV_1,\ldots,V_mV1​,…,Vm​ spans Rn\mathbb R^nRn at every point.

Formally: the Hörmander condition says that the vector fields {Vk}\{V_k\}{Vk​} and all their iterated Lie brackets [Vi,Vj][V_i,V_j][Vi​,Vj​], [Vi,[Vj,Vk]][V_i,[V_j,V_k]][Vi​,[Vj​,Vk​]], etc., span the full tangent space. This is a form of "geometric controllability."

Hörmander's theorem (informal)

If the Hörmander condition holds, the SDE's generator L\mathcal LL is hypoelliptic: the equation Lu=f\mathcal Lu = fLu=f has a smooth solution uuu whenever fff is smooth. Equivalently, the law of XtX_tXt​ has a smooth density for all t>0t > 0t>0.

This is remarkable: even if m<nm < nm<n (fewer BMs than dimensions), the process can spread into all dimensions through Lie bracket interactions. An SDE can be "essentially nnn-dimensional" even when driven by fewer Brownian motions.

14.6 Rough paths: an introduction​

The rough paths theory of Terry Lyons (1998) extends stochastic integration beyond semimartingales. The key insight: integrating against a rough path requires specifying not just the path but also its iterated integrals.

The problem. For a path X:[0,T]→RdX:[0,T]\to\mathbb R^dX:[0,T]→Rd of finite ppp-variation (p∈(2,3)p\in(2,3)p∈(2,3)), the naive Riemann sums ∑f(Xtk)(Xtk+1−Xtk)\sum f(X_{t_k})(X_{t_{k+1}}-X_{t_k})∑f(Xtk​​)(Xtk+1​​−Xtk​​) do not converge without additional data.

The additional data needed is the Lévy area:

Xs,tij=∫st(Xri−Xsi) dXrj.\mathbb X_{s,t}^{ij} = \int_s^t(X_r^i - X_s^i)\,dX_r^j.Xs,tij​=∫st​(Xri​−Xsi​)dXrj​.

This is the "area swept" by the path in the (i,j)(i,j)(i,j)-plane from sss to ttt.

Lyons' continuity theorem (informal)

For a rough path X=(X,X)\mathbf X = (X, \mathbb X)X=(X,X) (path plus its iterated integrals), the solution map

X↦Y,where Yt=y0+∫0tf(Ys) dXs,\mathbf X\mapsto Y,\quad\text{where }Y_t = y_0 + \int_0^t f(Y_s)\,d\mathbf X_s,X↦Y,where Yt​=y0​+∫0t​f(Ys​)dXs​,

is continuous in the rough path topology. Small perturbations of X\mathbf XX produce small perturbations of YYY.

Why this matters. Brownian motion, viewed as a rough path, needs to be enhanced by the Itô or Stratonovich Lévy area:

Ws,tIto,ij=∫st(Wri−Wsi) dWrj(Itoˆ)vsWs,tStrat,ij=∫st(Wri−Wsi)∘ dWrj(Stratonovich).\mathbb W^{{\rm Ito},ij}_{s,t} = \int_s^t(W_r^i-W_s^i)\,dW_r^j\quad(\text{Itô}) \quad\text{vs}\quad \mathbb W^{{\rm Strat},ij}_{s,t} = \int_s^t(W_r^i-W_s^i)\circ\,dW_r^j\quad(\text{Stratonovich}).Ws,tIto,ij​=∫st​(Wri​−Wsi​)dWrj​(Itoˆ)vsWs,tStrat,ij​=∫st​(Wri​−Wsi​)∘dWrj​(Stratonovich).

The difference is Ws,tStrat−Ws,tIto=12(t−s)δij\mathbb W^{{\rm Strat}}_{s,t} - \mathbb W^{{\rm Ito}}_{s,t} = \frac12(t-s)\delta_{ij}Ws,tStrat​−Ws,tIto​=21​(t−s)δij​. This is the Itô–Stratonovich correction, re-derived from the geometry of iterated integrals.

Rough paths as non-commutative geometry

The Lévy area is not redundant information — it captures how the two-dimensional projection of the path winds around the origin. When you compose two rough paths (the "composition" in path space), the Lévy area transforms non-trivially, like elements of a non-abelian Lie group. This is why rough path theory is deeply connected to the theory of free Lie algebras and the Chen–Strichartz expansion.

Practical impact: rough paths provide a unified framework for approximating SDEs driven by non-Brownian rough signals (fractional Brownian motion, wind, finance data), with rigorous error bounds.

14.7 Stochastic flows and diffeomorphisms​

For the SDE dXt=b(t,Xt) dt+σ(t,Xt) dWtdX_t = b(t,X_t)\,dt + \sigma(t,X_t)\,dW_tdXt​=b(t,Xt​)dt+σ(t,Xt​)dWt​ (in Stratonovich form), the map x↦Xt(x)x\mapsto X_t(x)x↦Xt​(x) (varying over initial conditions X0=xX_0 = xX0​=x) defines a stochastic flow of diffeomorphisms.

Existence of smooth flows

Under suitable smoothness and growth conditions on b,σb,\sigmab,σ, the solution map x↦Xt(x)x\mapsto X_t(x)x↦Xt​(x) is almost surely a CkC^kCk diffeomorphism for each ttt.

This means one can speak of the Jacobian Jt=∂Xt/∂X0J_t = \partial X_t/\partial X_0Jt​=∂Xt​/∂X0​, which satisfies the variational equation:

dJt=∂xb(t,Xt) Jt dt+∂xσ(t,Xt) Jt∘ dWt.dJ_t = \partial_x b(t,X_t)\,J_t\,dt + \partial_x\sigma(t,X_t)\,J_t\circ\,dW_t.dJt​=∂x​b(t,Xt​)Jt​dt+∂x​σ(t,Xt​)Jt​∘dWt​.

This is a linear Stratonovich SDE for JtJ_tJt​. Its solution describes how small perturbations to the initial condition evolve — the stochastic analogue of Lyapunov exponents.

Applications of stochastic flows in quantitative finance

Malliavin calculus (the calculus of variations for stochastic processes) uses the variation Jt=∂Xt/∂X0J_t = \partial X_t/\partial X_0Jt​=∂Xt​/∂X0​ to compute Greeks by the formula:

Δ=∂S0E[f(XT)]=E ⁣[f(XT)JTWTTS0].\Delta = \partial_{S_0}\mathbb E[f(X_T)] = \mathbb E\!\left[f(X_T)\frac{J_T W_T}{T S_0}\right].Δ=∂S0​​E[f(XT​)]=E[f(XT​)TS0​JT​WT​​].

This avoids finite-difference bumping and gives exact Greeks for complex payoffs.

Path sensitivity (algorithmic differentiation) in modern risk systems tracks Jacobians of stochastic flows through simulation, enabling fast computation of all Greeks simultaneously.

14.8 The Stroock–Varadhan support theorem​

For an SDE dX=b(X) dt+σ(X)∘dWdX = b(X)\,dt + \sigma(X)\circ dWdX=b(X)dt+σ(X)∘dW, the support theorem says that the support of the law of the full path X[0,T]X_{[0,T]}X[0,T]​ (as a measure on C([0,T],Rn)C([0,T],\mathbb R^n)C([0,T],Rn)) consists of exactly those paths that can be approximated by solutions with smooth control inputs:

supp(L(X))={yth:h∈C1([0,T],Rm)}‾,\text{supp}(\mathcal L(X)) = \overline{\{y_t^h: h\in C^1([0,T],\mathbb R^m)\}},supp(L(X))={yth​:h∈C1([0,T],Rm)}​,

where yhy^hyh solves the ODE dyth=b(yth) dt+σ(yth)ht′ dtdy_t^h = b(y_t^h)\,dt + \sigma(y_t^h)h'_t\,dtdyth​=b(yth​)dt+σ(yth​)ht′​dt.

In other words, the set of paths that actually occur (with positive probability) is the closure of the set of paths reachable by the deterministic system under all smooth controls. This identifies the support of the SDE with the reachable set of a controlled ODE.
